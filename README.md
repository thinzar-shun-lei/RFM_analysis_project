# Customer Segmentation with RFM Analysis

## Project Overview

This project is a mini customer analytics project built using a transactional dataset containing approximately 30,000 rows.

The project focuses on applying RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) analysis to understand customer purchasing behavior and determine how a business can take different actions based on customer characteristics and value.

The analysis transforms transaction-level data into customer-level metrics, assigns RFM scores using quintiles, segments customers based on their purchasing behavior, and develops business strategies and recommended actions for each customer segment.

The primary business objective is to answer:

- Who are the most valuable customers?
- Which customers are highly engaged?
- Which customers have the potential to become loyal customers?
- Which valuable customers may be at risk of becoming inactive?
- Which customers are inactive or hibernating?
- What actions should the business take for each customer segment?

# Data Cleaning and Transformation

Data preparation was performed using Power Query in Power BI.

The following transformations were applied to prepare the dataset for analysis.

### 1. Split Mixed IDs

The original dataset contained mixed identifiers in the same field.

The mixed ID field was split into separate:

- Transaction ID
- Customer ID

This allowed transactions and customers to be analyzed independently.

### 2. Split Region and Date

The Region and Date information was separated into individual fields to ensure that geographic and time-based analysis could be performed correctly.

### 3. Remove Null Values

Null and missing values that could affect the analysis were identified and removed where appropriate.

### 4. Change Date and required Data Type

Date fields were converted to the appropriate date data type using Power Query's locale settings to ensure that dates were interpreted correctly.

# RFM Analysis

RFM analysis was used to evaluate customer purchasing behavior based on three dimensions:

Recency - Number of days since the customer's most recent order [How recently did the customer purchase?]
Frequency - Number of repeat orders [How frequently does the customer purchase?]
Monetary - Total revenue generated by the customer [How much value does the customer generate?]

---

# RFM Quintile Scoring

To make the three RFM metrics comparable, customers were divided into five groups using quintile-based scoring.

Each metric was assigned a score from 1 to 5.

Each quintile represents approximately 20% of the customer population.

### RFM Score Interpretation

| Score | General Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Highest-performing group |
| 4 | High-performing group |
| 3 | Middle group |
| 2 | Low-performing group |
| 1 | Lowest-performing group |

### Recency Scoring

Recency is treated differently from Frequency and Monetary because a lower number of days is better.

Therefore:

- Lower Recency = Higher R Score
- Higher Recency = Lower R Score

### Frequency Scoring

Higher purchase frequency receives a higher F Score.

### Monetary Scoring

Higher customer revenue receives a higher M Score.

The resulting RFM scores allow customers with different purchasing behaviors to be compared consistently.

# Customer Segmentation

Customers were segmented based on their R, F, and M scores.

The project uses seven customer segments:

1. Champions
2. Loyal Customers
3. High-Value Customers
4. Potential Loyalists
5. New Customers
6. At Risk Customers - Need Attention
7. Lost / Hibernating Customers

## Customer Segmentation Criteria using R, F, M Score

| Priority | Customer Segment | R Score | F Score | M Score | Business Interpretation |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | **Champions** | ≥ 4 | ≥ 4 | ≥ 4 | Recent, frequent, and high-value customers |
| 2 | **Loyal Customers** | ≥ 3 | ≥ 4 | ≥ 1 | Frequent and reasonably recent customers |
| 3 | **High-Value Customers** | ≥ 3 | ≥ 3 | ≥ 4 | Customers with high monetary value |
| 4 | **Potential Loyalists** | ≥ 3 | ≥ 3 | ≥ 1 | Relatively recent customers with moderate-to-high engagement |
| 5 | **New Customers** | ≥ 4 | 1–2 | Any | Recent customers with low purchase frequency |
| 6 | **At Risk Customers - Need Attention** | ≤ 3 | ≥ 2 | ≥ 2 | Less recent customers who still demonstrate meaningful value or engagement |
| 7 | **Lost / Hibernating Customers** | ≤ 3 | ≥ 1 | Any | Customers with declining or inactive engagement |

### Segmentation Logic

The criteria are evaluated from top to bottom using DAX `SWITCH(TRUE())`. Because some criteria overlap, the first matching condition determines the customer's segment.

For example, a customer with R = 5, F = 5, and M = 5 meets both the **Champions** and **Loyal Customers** criteria, but is classified as **Champions** because that condition has higher priority.

## DAX used in Analysis

### 1. Days Since Last Order

Calculates the number of days between a customer's most recent order and a fixed reference date. This measure is used as the foundation for calculating **Recency** in the RFM analysis.

```DAX
DaysSinceLastOrder =  
VAR setDate = "10-Jul-2025" 
VAR lastOrderDate = MAX('raw_rfm_sales_transactions_30000 csv'[Date]) 
VAR daysSinceLastOrder = DATEDIFF(lastOrderDate,setDate,DAY) 
RETURN daysSinceLastOrder
```
---

### 2. Recency Score

Converts the number of days since a customer's last order into a **Recency score from 1 to 5**.

The measure first calculates the **20th, 40th, 60th, and 80th percentiles** of customers' days since their last order. `SWITCH(TRUE())` then assigns scores based on these percentile thresholds.

* **5** → Most recent customers
* **4** → Relatively recent customers
* **3** → Average recency
* **2** → Less recent customers
* **1** → Least recent customers

```DAX
Recency = 

VAR Q20 = ROUND(PERCENTILEX.INC(ALL('raw_rfm_sales_transactions_30000 csv'[Customer Id]),[DaysSinceLastOrder],0.2),0) 
VAR Q40 = ROUND(PERCENTILEX.INC(ALL('raw_rfm_sales_transactions_30000 csv'[Customer Id]),[DaysSinceLastOrder],0.4),0) 
VAR Q60 = ROUND(PERCENTILEX.INC(ALL('raw_rfm_sales_transactions_30000 csv'[Customer Id]),[DaysSinceLastOrder],0.6),0) 
VAR Q80 = ROUND(PERCENTILEX.INC(ALL('raw_rfm_sales_transactions_30000 csv'[Customer Id]),[DaysSinceLastOrder],0.8),0) 

RETURN  
SWITCH( 
    TRUE(), 
    [DaysSinceLastOrder] <= Q20, 5, 
    [DaysSinceLastOrder] <= Q40, 4, 
    [DaysSinceLastOrder] <= Q60, 3, 
    [DaysSinceLastOrder] <= Q80, 2, 
    1
)
```

---

### 3. Customer Segmentation

Classifies customers into meaningful business segments using their **Recency (R), Frequency (F), and Monetary (M) scores**.

```DAX
Customer Segmentation =  
VAR R = [Recency] 
VAR F = [Frequency] 
VAR M = [Monetary] 
 
RETURN 
SWITCH( 
    TRUE(), 
    (R >= 4 && F >= 4 && M >= 4), "🏆Champions", 
    (R >= 3 && F >= 4 && M >= 1), "💰Loyal Customers", 
    (R >= 3 && F >= 3 && M >= 4), "💸High-Value Customers", 
    (R >= 3 && F >= 3 && M >= 1), "🌱Potential Loyalists", 
    (R >= 4 && (F = 1 || F = 2)), "🆕New Customers", 
    (R <= 3 && F >= 2 && M >= 2), "🚨At Risk Customers - Need Attention", 
    (R <= 3 && F >= 1), "📉Lost/ Hibernating Customers", 
    "Others"
)
```

## Customer Segment Insights & Recommendations

- **Loyal Customers (24.02%)** represent the largest segment. They purchase frequently and are reasonably recent, making them strong candidates for loyalty programs, upselling, premium products, and review or feedback campaigns.

- **At Risk Customers (22.35%)** form another major segment. Although they have demonstrated value or engagement in the past, their recent activity has declined. Personalized discounts, win-back campaigns, reminders, and direct outreach can help reactivate them.

- **Lost / Hibernating Customers (14.91%)** are relatively inactive and have lower value or frequency. The business should use low-cost reactivation campaigns and avoid spending excessive resources on customers who are unlikely to return.

- **Potential Loyalists (11.86%)** are relatively recent customers with moderate purchasing frequency. Second- or third-purchase incentives, product recommendations, and bundled offers can help convert them into loyal customers.

- **New Customers (11.21%)** have made recent purchases but have low frequency. Onboarding, personalized product recommendations, and second-purchase incentives can help establish a stronger relationship.

- **Champions (10.65%)** are recent, frequent, and high-value customers. They should be prioritized for retention and rewards through VIP programs, early access to premium products, cross-selling, and referral programs.

- **High-Value Customers (5%)** generate significant monetary value. The business should focus on protecting this revenue through premium service, increasing purchase frequency, and maintaining strong long-term relationships.

### Overall Business Recommendation

The segmentation highlights three major priorities:

1. **Retain and reward valuable customers** — especially Champions, Loyal Customers, and High-Value Customers.
2. **Reactivate customers showing signs of churn** — particularly At Risk and Lost / Hibernating Customers.
3. **Develop newer customers into loyal customers** — particularly New Customers and Potential Loyalists.
